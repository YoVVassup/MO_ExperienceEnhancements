# Feature Showcase — Environment Control

![alt text](imgs/img_esc_xs.png)


## Environment control
MO maps are overall rather dark, with average lighting around 0.8.
The lighting settings let you brighten overly dark maps and make some atrocious lighting less atrocious.
The presets here are the same presets as in the map editor.

Environment: overall brightness, applied to buildings and units.
Level: how much brightness changes with altitude; a negative value gives the "cliffs go black" indoor look.
Ground: ground brightness offset.
Red/Green/Blue: the overall color tint.

### Background knowledge
The game's lighting actually comes in two kinds.

One I call "default lighting":
the look you get the moment you enter the map, units and buildings stand out more
(buildings and units are not tinted, hence more visible, but under strong lighting it can look too harsh).
You can darken the ground and brighten units, or the other way around, for fancy effects; light-emitting
buildings and such behave normally.

The other is "trigger lighting":
buildings and units blend into the environment better and are less conspicuous at the same values
(buildings and units are tinted, the style is more unified, but colors are less prominent and the color
range is narrow).
High-contrast tricks don't work; lights and glowing buildings break and need separate fixes.
Occasionally, when the default-lighting tricks were tuned badly, trigger lighting looks better.

on the left is the default eye-catching lighting, in the middle the atmospheric trigger lighting (colors
are lost), on the right the fix + blended lighting.

### Lighting fix
The jagged ground is caused by trigger lighting; recoloring can fix it.
![alt text](imgs/img_fix_li2.png)
Lights/radiation-type lighting is painted on the ground, which is why trigger lighting has this problem.
Fixing the lighting means fixing the ground color along with it, which loses some of the atmosphere the
trigger provides.
After the fix the picture generally has more, brighter colors, so compared to the real trigger colors
there is an actual color shift.
Trigger-colored buildings and units will usually look a bit darker (because the environment is more
vivid).

That is why a blend ratio was added: you get both the brightness and the tinting, and you can switch
between tinting modes.


### Game environment and weather
You can freely change unreasonable in-game lighting, or make lighting suited for screenshots.
Weather control was added.
You can set texture weather, i.e. in-game rain and snow.
Or show rain/snow as particle effects. Rain has rain sounds, snow has wind sounds, lightning has thunder.

Cloud shadows or fog display were added to enhance the atmosphere.
Cloud shadows are generally fine; fog can look a bit odd.
![alt text](imgs/img_fix_we.jpg)
